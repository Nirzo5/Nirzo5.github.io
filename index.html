Academic project · Intro to Deep Learning, Tel Aviv University · Grade 95

Bringing Van Gogh back to life: a classifier that judges its own art

A three-person team project. We fine-tuned CNNs to recognize Van Gogh's paintings, then used that classifier as the judge in an automated search for the neural style transfer settings that produce the most convincing “Van Gogh”.

Step 1 · Classify

A Van Gogh detector

Fine-tuned VGG-19 and AlexNet on 6,450 Post-Impressionist paintings (15.6% Van Gogh), with a stratified 70/15/15 split, augmentation, per-architecture partial unfreezing and early stopping. Optuna searched learning rate, batch size, optimizer and dropout; every run tracked in Weights & Biases.

Step 2 · Stylize

Neural style transfer from scratch

A generic style transfer function: content loss on deep feature maps plus a Gram-matrix style loss, normalized per layer so no single layer dominates. Works with either network as the feature extractor, with justified content and style layer choices.

Step 3 · Judge

The classifier as the objective

Defined a new optimization target: Optuna tuned the content and style weights to maximize the classifier's “Van Gogh” probability for the generated image. Then applied it to 20 photos with 5+ Van Gogh paintings, using both networks as engine and as judge.

94%

test accuracy for both fine-tuned models, AUC-ROC 0.97

0.78

average “Van Gogh” probability of the optimized generated images

0.72 vs 0.41

judge score for images painted with VGG-19 vs AlexNet as the engine

44×

GPU speedup over CPU for training, benchmarked on a Tesla T4

Key insight

The better critic isn't always the better painter. AlexNet edged VGG-19 on classification, but VGG-19's deeper feature hierarchy produced images that both judges rated far more “Van Gogh”.
